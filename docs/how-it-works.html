How it works · plain language

One system that runs the whole club.

Enquiries, batches, attendance, fees, pool bookings and the café — in one place, on a phone, for everyone who works there and everyone who pays for it.

The idea in one paragraph

Right now, a club runs on four different things

A paper register for attendance. A WhatsApp group for parents. An Excel sheet for fees. A notebook for who took a salary advance. And the owner's memory for everything else. Nothing talks to anything else, so the owner never actually knows how much money is owed, what the month really cost in staff wages, which batches are half empty, or which member is about to quit.

This replaces all four with one system — and, importantly, each person only ever sees the part that concerns them.

Who uses it

Five people, five completely different screens

This is the part most competitors get wrong. They give the owner, the coach and the parent the same dashboard. A coach standing at the pool at 6 AM does not need a revenue chart.

The flow

A normal day at the club

Nobody sits down to "use the software." It happens in thirty-second moments across the day. Here is one Saturday.

6:40 AM
Coach

Checks herself in

One tap on arrival, or a scan of the QR code at the gate. That single tap is what her salary will be calculated from at the end of the month — nobody has to remember it, and nobody has to argue about it later.

6:45 AM
Coach

Opens the register on her phone

Fourteen names for the 7 AM beginners batch. She taps present or absent as each child arrives. The pool has no signal — it saves anyway and syncs when she walks back to the office.

If they don't pay

A polite reminder goes at three days, then seven, then fourteen, then thirty. Each one is automatic. The owner is only pulled in when it genuinely needs a human.

Cash still works

A lot of money in this business is cash at the counter. It gets recorded against the right member, by the right staff member, and reconciled at the end of the day. If the software could not handle cash, the register would survive.

Worth saying plainly: we take no percentage of the club's fees. The main competitor charges 2% on every online payment, on rails that cost them nothing. For a club collecting ₹4 lakh a month, that is ₹8,000 a month going to a middleman.

The other half of the money

Paying the staff

Wages are usually the single biggest expense a club has. Today it lives in a notebook: how many sessions did each coach take, who took an advance last month, who was on leave. It gets worked out by hand on the first of the month, and it is the most common thing for a club and its coaches to argue about.

The system already knows almost all of it — because it recorded every session, every check-in and every substitution as it happened.

Nothing is paid automatically

The system prepares the sheet. A human always looks at it, changes what needs changing, and approves it. Once approved it is locked — a correction goes into next month rather than quietly rewriting history.

Every number can be opened up

When a coach says "I took more sessions than that," the owner taps the line and sees the exact thirty-four sessions, with dates. The argument ends in ten seconds instead of ten minutes.

Advances are tracked properly

Staff take advances against salary — that is normal and it is not going away. The system records what was given, recovers it in instalments, and shows what is still outstanding. No more notebook.

Leave has a balance

Staff apply, the manager approves, the roster updates, and if a coach is on leave the system flags which batches are left uncovered — before the morning it happens.

₹800 × 22

Here is the thing this unlocks. Once the system knows what the coach costs and what the batch earns, it can answer a question no club owner can answer today: which batches actually make money?

A 6 PM batch with four students paying ₹3,000 a month brings in ₹12,000. The coach taking it twenty-two times at ₹800 a session costs ₹17,600. That batch loses ₹5,600 every month, and right now nobody would ever notice.

Merge it into the 7 PM batch, or fill it — either way, the owner can only decide once they can see it.

One thing this deliberately does not do: the formal government side of payroll — provident fund, ESI, tax deduction, Form 16. That is a specialist product with real legal risk, and most clubs this size are below the size where it is required. Instead the system hands clean monthly figures to whatever the club's accountant already uses.

The clever bit

One product, many clubs, each set up differently

A swimming club with a café needs different things from a football academy. Most software companies solve this by building a slightly different version for each customer, which becomes impossible to maintain by about the fifth customer.

Instead, everything is built once and turned on or off per club — like a switchboard. Adding an unusual customer takes minutes, not a new version of the software.

Each club's data is separate

One club can never see another club's members, money or records. This is enforced by the database itself, not just by the app.

Each club looks like itself

Its own logo and colours, and its own words — "swimmer" for one club, "player" for another, "member" for a third.

Everyone gets every improvement

Because there is only one system, a fix or a new feature reaches all clubs at once.
